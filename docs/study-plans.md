# Study plans

Two plans: one for a week, one for when the invitation has already arrived. Both start with the [15-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md), so you know which formats need the most work. Expect 30 to 60 minutes a day on the 7-day plan.

SJTs reward practice differently from maths or verbal tests. You are not learning content. You are training a habit: reading each scenario for the role, the risk and the rule, then checking every option against the same five questions. Short, regular sessions build that habit faster than one long cram.

## 7-day plan

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Read the [test overview](test-overview.md). Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). Note your two weakest formats. Find out which format your own test uses. |
| 2 | The five checks | Read the [most and least guide](sections/most-and-least-effective.md) in full and do all 5 items. Learn the five checks until you can say them without looking. |
| 3 | Ranking | Read the [ranking guide](sections/ranking-responses.md) and do all 5 items. For every miss, note whether it was at the top, the bottom or in the middle. |
| 4 | Rating | Read the [rating effectiveness](sections/rating-effectiveness.md) and [rating importance](sections/rating-importance.md) guides. Do both item sets. Applying to the Civil Service? Read the [Civil Service guide](civil-service.md) today. |
| 5 | First timed run | Take a full timed practice set, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/sjt?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=xt9w5q&utm_content=situational-judgement-test). Score it by format. For the Civil Service, also try the official practice test linked in the [Civil Service guide](civil-service.md#practise-with-the-official-tools). |
| 6 | Fix the weakest format | Go back to the traps list for your weakest format. Redo its items without looking at the answers. Then do at least 15 fresh items in that format. |
| 7 | Second run and rest | Do a second timed set in the morning. Compare it with day 5. Read the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md). Stop by early evening. |

### How to review a missed item

For each miss, answer three questions:

1. **Which check did I get wrong?** Problem, people, rules, role or timing.
2. **What in the scenario should have told me?** Name the words: "the policy says", "you are a student", "the patient is conscious".
3. **What will I look for next time?** One short habit, such as "find the stated rule before reading the options".

Keep the answers in one list. By day 6 you will usually see the same one or two habits causing most misses. For many people it is either rewarding heroics outside their role, or choosing "do nothing" as the worst option when another option causes harm.

## 2-day plan

For when the link has arrived and the deadline is close. GOV.UK advises taking the Civil Service test as early as possible so you have time to sort out any technical or access problems, and to send any queries at least two working days before the deadline.

### Day 1: learn the method

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 min | Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| 2 | 15 min | Read "The scoring frame used in these notes" and "Traps that cost points" in the [most and least guide](sections/most-and-least-effective.md). |
| 3 | 30 min | Do the practice items for the format your real test uses. Read every explanation, including the ones you got right. |
| 4 | 30 min | Take a timed practice set, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/sjt?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=xt9w5q&utm_content=situational-judgement-test). |
| 5 | 15 min | Review every miss with the three questions above. |

### Day 2: sit the test

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 min | Redo yesterday's misses without the answers. |
| 2 | 10 min | Read the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md). |
| 3 | 10 min | Warm up with five items in your test's format. |
| 4 | Test | Sit the real test in a quiet place, at a time when you are usually alert. |

If you only have one evening, do day 1 blocks 1, 2 and 4, then sit the test the next day.

## What not to do

- **Do not try to guess what the employer "wants to hear".** The options are written so that the people-pleasing answer is often wrong. Use the five checks.
- **Do not hunt for leaked keys.** Keys differ by employer and role, and using them may break the test rules. They also teach nothing for the interview that often follows.
- **Do not ask anyone for help during the real test.** GOV.UK says not to ask anyone for input on your Civil Service answers. Most employers have the same rule.
- **Do not cram.** A tired reader misses the one line in the scenario that changes the answer.
