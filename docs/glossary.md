# Glossary

Terms you will meet in SJT instructions, results and these notes.

**Appropriateness rating.** A rating format, used in the UCAT, where you judge how appropriate an action is, from "a very appropriate thing to do" to "a very inappropriate thing to do".

**Band.** The way the UCAT reports SJT results, from Band 1 (highest) to Band 4.

**Behaviours (Civil Service).** The behaviours the Civil Service assesses when it recruits. One of the five elements of Success Profiles, and what the Civil Service Judgement Test measures.

**Competency framework.** An employer's list of the qualities it wants in a role, such as teamwork or integrity. Employer SJTs are usually keyed to one.

**Counterproductive.** The lowest rating in the Civil Service Judgement Test. In these notes: an action that makes the situation worse.

**CSJT.** The Civil Service Judgement Test.

**Effective / Fairly Effective / Ineffective.** The other three Civil Service ratings. See the [rating effectiveness guide](sections/rating-effectiveness.md#the-scoring-frame-used-in-these-notes) for how these notes interpret them.

**Escalate.** To pass a problem to someone with more authority or expertise, such as a manager, supervisor or safeguarding lead.

**Expert panel.** A group of experienced people whose agreed judgements form the answer key. The UCAT's band descriptions compare candidates with an expert panel.

**Five checks.** The frame used throughout these notes: problem, people, rules, role and timing.

**Importance rating.** A format, used in the UCAT, where you rate how much a consideration should matter to a decision, from "very important" to "not important at all".

**Key.** The set of answers a test treats as correct, or as most correct.

**Most and least.** A format where you pick the best and the worst option from a list.

**Partial credit.** Marks given for an answer close to the key. The UCAT says it gives partial marks when your response is close to the correct answer.

**Percentile.** The share of a comparison group you scored better than. The Civil Service Judgement Test reports a percentile.

**Ranking.** A format where you put options in order from most to least effective.

**Safeguarding.** Protecting children and vulnerable adults from harm. Many care, health and education scenarios turn on it.

**Scenario.** The short description of a situation that each SJT item is built on.

**Self-assessment (CSJT).** Part 1 of the Civil Service Judgement Test, where you rate how ambitious, motivated and flexible you are at work. It makes up 15% of the overall score.

**SJT.** Situational judgement test.

**Success Profiles.** The framework the Civil Service uses to recruit. Behaviours are one of its five elements.

**UCAT.** The University Clinical Aptitude Test, used by UK medical and dental schools. Situational Judgement is one of its subtests.
