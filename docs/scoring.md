# How SJT scoring works

Short version: your answers are compared with a key agreed by people who know the job, near misses often earn partial credit, and your total is reported against other candidates. How that total is shown depends on the test. The Civil Service Judgement Test gives a percentile. The UCAT gives a band from 1 to 4.

## Where the key comes from

In general, SJT designers write scenarios from real situations in the job, then ask a panel of experienced people to rate the responses. Where the panel agrees strongly, that becomes the key. This is our general description of how SJTs are built, not a statement about any one publisher's process. The UCAT's own band descriptions do compare candidates' judgement with an expert panel's.

So the key is not about what is "nice" or what you personally prefer. It is about what experienced people in that role consider good practice.

## Partial credit

Most SJTs do not treat an answer as simply right or wrong:

- The **UCAT** says it gives full marks for a question if your response matches the correct answer, and partial marks if your response is close to the correct answer. It does not publish the exact mark values.
- **Rating scales** naturally allow this: rating an action "Fairly Effective" when the key says "Effective" is a smaller miss than rating it "Counterproductive".
- **Ranking items**, in tests that use them, often score how close each option is to its keyed position. The scoring in these notes gives one mark per option in the right place, which is simpler than most real tests.

The practical lesson: never leave an item blank, and when unsure between two adjacent labels, pick one of them. A one-step miss usually still scores something.

## Civil Service Judgement Test: percentiles

GOV.UK says:

- Your score is a **percentile**, compared with a representative group of applicants. A percentile of 44 means you scored better than 44% of that group.
- The **self-assessment** in Part 1 makes up **15%** of your overall score.
- **Recruiters set the pass mark** and may raise it after the deadline. Meeting the minimum does not guarantee an invitation to the next stage.
- **Feedback** is generated automatically. You will not get feedback on specific answers.
- If you do not pass, you can **take the test again in future applications**.

More detail is in the [Civil Service guide](civil-service.md).

## UCAT SJT: bands

The UCAT reports the Situational Judgement result as a band, with Band 1 the highest. In the UCAT's own words, in short:

| Band | What it means |
|---|---|
| Band 1 | An excellent level of performance, showing similar judgement to the expert panel in most cases |
| Band 2 | A good, solid level of performance, showing appropriate judgement frequently |
| Band 3 | A modest level of performance, with appropriate judgement for some questions and substantial differences for others |
| Band 4 | A low level of performance, with judgement differing substantially in many cases |

Source: [UCAT: Test format and scoring](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/about-ucat/test-format-and-scoring/), checked 24 November 2026.

The UCAT does not publish a pass mark. Check the admissions pages of every course you apply to for how they use the SJT band.

## What counts as a good score

- **Civil Service:** there is no single number. The pass mark depends on the job and the applicant pool, and can move after the deadline. A higher percentile simply means you did better than more of the comparison group.
- **UCAT:** Band 1 is the top band, and Band 2 is described as good, solid performance. Beyond that, check how the universities you apply to use the band.
- **Employer SJTs:** employers rarely publish cut-offs. Treat numbers you see quoted online with caution.

What you can control, as our study guidance:

- **Aim for most of your misses to be one step away.** On rating items in these notes, if almost all your errors are adjacent labels, your judgement is close to the key.
- **Clear the "least" picks.** Choosing an option that causes harm as best, or as acceptable, is the costliest kind of error.
- **Score 80% or more on well-made practice**, under realistic timing, before test day. This is our target, not a published standard.

## Why your score can vary between tests

Each SJT is keyed to its own role and framework. A response that is best for a hospital ward might be second best for a sales team, because the risks and rules differ. If your scores vary between employers' tests, that is normal. Keep using the five checks, and read every scenario for the role and the rule it gives you.
