# FAQ

Questions people really search for about situational judgement tests, answered from official sources where a fact is involved. The questions come from Google's "People also ask" results for UK searches on "situational judgement test" in November 2026, plus common questions about the two named tests in these notes.

## What is a situational judgement test?

A test that shows you realistic work situations and asks how you should respond. You might pick the best and worst option, rank options, or rate each one. It measures judgement rather than knowledge. See [Situational judgement tests explained](test-overview.md).

## What are situational questions in an exam?

They are questions built around a short scenario: "You are a team member and this happens. What should you do?" In an SJT the scenario gives you your role, the problem and sometimes a rule, and the answer options are possible actions or factors to weigh. The four common formats each have a guide in these notes, starting with [most and least effective](sections/most-and-least-effective.md).

## Can you fail a situational judgement test?

Yes, where the employer sets a cut-off. GOV.UK says recruiters set the pass mark for the Civil Service Judgement Test, may raise it after the deadline, and that meeting the minimum does not guarantee you will move to the next stage. The UCAT does not report a pass or fail: it gives a band from 1 to 4.

## What is a good score on the situational judgement test in the UK?

It depends on the test:

- **Civil Service Judgement Test:** a percentile against other applicants. There is no fixed good score, because the pass mark depends on the job and can change after the deadline.
- **UCAT SJT:** Band 1 is the highest and is described as an excellent level of performance. Band 2 is described as good, solid performance.

See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## What percentage of candidates get Band 1 in the UCAT SJT?

We have not quoted a figure. The share changes from year to year, and these notes only use numbers we can check on the official UCAT site. If you need it for a given year, look on the [UCAT website](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/). What the UCAT does publish is what each band means: Band 1 shows judgement similar to the expert panel in most cases.

## Is ChatGPT good at situational judgement tests?

AI chatbots can often give sensible-sounding answers to SJT-style questions, because SJT keys reward widely shared ideas of good practice. But using one during a real test is a different matter. GOV.UK says not to ask anyone for input on your Civil Service Judgement Test answers, and most employers' rules are similar. An SJT is also often followed by an interview about the same qualities, so outside help on the test leaves you less ready for the next stage.

If you want to use AI while studying, use it to explain why a keyed answer is right after you have committed to your own, not to answer for you. Then check the reasoning against the five checks in these notes.

## Can you prepare for a situational judgement test?

Yes. You cannot learn a key, but you can learn the pattern behind good answers and get used to the formats. The [study plans](study-plans.md) give a 7-day and a 2-day route.

## Is the Civil Service Judgement Test timed?

No. GOV.UK says it is not timed, that most people take two to four minutes per scenario, and to allow at least an hour. See the [Civil Service guide](civil-service.md#timing).

## How long is the UCAT SJT?

69 questions in 26 minutes of working time, plus 1 minute 30 seconds of instructions, according to the UCAT. Each scenario has up to six questions.

## Do I need medical knowledge for the UCAT SJT?

No. The UCAT says no medical knowledge is needed. The scenarios are set in medical and dental training, but they test judgement, such as integrity, perspective taking and teamwork.

## Can I retake the Civil Service Judgement Test?

GOV.UK says that if you do not pass, you can take it again in future applications. Within one application, you can reopen the browser only twice after you first start, and launch the test three times in total, so take care with your setup.

## Is there an official practice test?

For the Civil Service, yes: GOV.UK links an unscored [CSJT practice test](https://eubusiness.panpowered.com/Civil_Service_Practice/CSJT_practice). The UCAT publishes question tutorials on its [preparation pages](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/prepare/question-tutorials/). For other employers, check your invitation.

## Why did I get a low score when my answers seemed sensible?

Most SJT options are written to seem sensible. The usual reasons for a lower score are: rewarding actions that are kind but outside your role, choosing "do nothing" as the worst option when another option causes harm, and missing a rule stated in the scenario. The traps lists in each format guide cover these.

## Do employers use the same SJT?

No. Each employer or admissions body uses its own test, keyed to its own role and values. That is why the method in these notes focuses on the reasoning behind good answers rather than on any one test's content.
