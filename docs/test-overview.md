# Situational judgement tests explained

A situational judgement test (SJT) puts you in realistic work situations and asks how you would, or should, respond. There are no sums and no grammar rules. What is being tested is judgement: whether your instincts match those of people who do the job well.

SJTs are not one test from one publisher. Every employer or admissions body builds or buys its own, keyed to its own idea of good behaviour. That is why these notes take facts only from the organisations that run named tests, and teach a method that works across all of them.

## Quick facts

Two widely taken SJTs in the UK, side by side:

| | UK Civil Service Judgement Test | UCAT Situational Judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | UK Civil Service | UCAT Consortium |
| Who takes it | Applicants for many Civil Service jobs | Applicants to UK medical and dental degrees |
| Length | Not timed. GOV.UK says most people take 2 to 4 minutes per scenario and to allow at least an hour | 69 questions, 26 minutes, plus 1 minute 30 seconds of instructions |
| Formats | A self-assessment (rating how ambitious, motivated and flexible you are at work), then scenarios with four actions each to rate | Rating appropriateness, rating importance, and choosing the most and least appropriate of three options |
| Scale | Counterproductive, Ineffective, Fairly Effective, Effective | Very appropriate to very inappropriate, or Very important to Not important at all |
| Media | Video, text or a mix, depending on the job | Text scenarios |
| Result | A percentile against a representative group of applicants | Band 1 (highest) to Band 4 |
| Pass mark | Set by the recruiter, and can be raised after the deadline | None published by the UCAT. Check how each university you apply to uses the band |

Sources: [GOV.UK: Preparing for the Civil Service Judgement Test](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/preparing-for-the-new-civil-service-judgement-test), last updated 24 May 2024, and [UCAT: Test format and scoring](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/about-ucat/test-format-and-scoring/), checked 24 November 2026. The UCAT says its tutorial information applies to the 2026 testing cycle and may change for 2027.

## What an SJT is measuring

Each test lists different qualities, but they overlap a lot:

- The **Civil Service Judgement Test** measures judgement and decision-making against the Civil Service Behaviours, which are one of the five elements of the Civil Service's Success Profiles framework.
- The **UCAT SJT** measures your capacity to understand real-world situations and to identify the critical factors and appropriate behaviour. It looks at integrity, perspective taking, team involvement, resilience and adaptability, and needs no medical knowledge.
- **Employer SJTs** on graduate schemes and in retail, care, banking and elsewhere are usually built around the employer's own values or competency framework.

Underneath, almost every keyed answer rewards the same habits: deal with the real problem, keep people safe, be honest, follow the rules that apply, know the limits of your role, and act in good time. These notes call them the five checks.

## The four formats

| Format | You do | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Most and least effective | Pick the best and worst option from three to five | [Most and least](sections/most-and-least-effective.md) |
| Ranking | Put four options in order, best to worst | [Ranking](sections/ranking-responses.md) |
| Rating effectiveness | Rate each action on its own, on a four-point scale | [Rating effectiveness](sections/rating-effectiveness.md) |
| Rating importance | Rate how much each factor should weigh in a decision | [Rating importance](sections/rating-importance.md) |

A single test may use one format or several. The Civil Service test rates actions. The UCAT mixes rating appropriateness, rating importance and most-and-least. Employer tests vary, so read your invitation and the on-screen instructions.

## How answers are keyed

You will not find the key for any real SJT, and you do not need it. In general, test designers write scenarios from real incidents in the job and then ask a panel of experienced people to rate the responses. The keyed answer is what that panel agrees on. The UCAT's band descriptions, for example, compare candidates' judgement with that of an expert panel.

Two things follow:

1. **The key reflects good practice in the role, not personal taste.** Answer as a capable person doing that job well, not as you might on a tired day.
2. **Some answers earn partial credit.** The UCAT says it gives full marks when your response matches the correct answer and partial marks when it is close. A near miss still scores.

## The one rule that runs through every SJT

**Judge the response by what it does, not by how it sounds.** Options are written to sound reasonable. The best one solves the actual problem in the right way. The worst one causes harm, often while sounding kind or decisive. If you take one habit from these notes, make it the five checks in the [most and least guide](sections/most-and-least-effective.md#the-scoring-frame-used-in-these-notes).
