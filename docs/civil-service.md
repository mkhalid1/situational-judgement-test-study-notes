# For Civil Service applicants

The Civil Service Judgement Test (CSJT) is one of the most widely taken SJTs in the UK, and it has some features that general SJT advice misses: a self-assessment that counts towards your score, untimed scenarios, and firm limits on how many times you can open the test. This page collects what GOV.UK says, and what it means for how you prepare.

All facts on this page come from [GOV.UK: Preparing for the Civil Service Judgement Test](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/preparing-for-the-new-civil-service-judgement-test), last updated 24 May 2024 and checked 24 November 2026. Our own advice is marked as guidance.

## Where it fits

The CSJT measures judgement and decision-making against the **Civil Service Behaviours**. Behaviours are one of the five elements of the **Success Profiles** framework that the Civil Service uses to recruit.

You only see scenarios that are relevant to the level of the job you applied for, and the scenarios may be in video, text or a mix, depending on the job.

**Guidance:** the job advert lists the Behaviours that role will assess. Read them before you start. You do not need to memorise definitions, but knowing what the role values helps you read each scenario the way the panel will.

## Part 1: the self-assessment

Before the scenarios, you rate how ambitious, motivated and flexible you are at work. This part makes up **15% of your overall score**.

GOV.UK says to answer it honestly, because you may be asked about it at interview.

**Guidance:** answer the way you would be happy to back up with an example. If you rate yourself very highly on flexibility, have a story ready about a time you adapted quickly. That is good preparation for the interview anyway.

## Part 2: the scenarios

You read workplace scenarios. Each has **four actions**, and you rate each action as:

| Rating | In these notes |
|---|---|
| Effective | Passes all five checks |
| Fairly Effective | Helps, but misses a step, a person or the best timing |
| Ineffective | Does not solve the problem, but does not make it worse |
| Counterproductive | Makes the situation worse |

There are **three scenarios per Behaviour**, with four actions each. Rate each action on its own: several actions can get the same rating.

The method and 5 practice items for this format are in the [rating effectiveness guide](sections/rating-effectiveness.md). The Civil Service scale is used in its first scenario.

## Timing

The CSJT is **not timed**. Most people take **two to four minutes per scenario**, and GOV.UK suggests allowing **at least an hour**.

**Guidance:** untimed does not mean unlimited attention. Long sessions get tiring, and tired readers miss details. Pick a time when you have an unbroken hour and a half, and take a short pause between scenarios if you need one.

## Launch limits: read this before you start

GOV.UK sets out some limits that are easy to miss:

- If you close the browser, you can reopen it where you left off, but **only twice after the first start**.
- If you lose access, sign in to your application centre to relaunch the test. You can **launch it three times in total**.
- Practice questions appear at the start of each test.

**Guidance:** treat every launch as precious. Use a stable internet connection, close other tabs, plug in your laptop, and do not start the test on a phone while travelling.

## Accessibility

GOV.UK says subtitles, British Sign Language translations and written transcripts are available, and that videos can be replayed. If you need an adjustment beyond that, contact the recruiter early. GOV.UK asks for queries at least two working days before the deadline.

## Results and retakes

- Your score is a **percentile** compared with a representative group of applicants.
- **Recruiters set the pass mark** and may raise it after the deadline.
- Meeting the minimum **does not guarantee** an invitation to the next stage.
- Feedback is generated automatically. You will **not get feedback on specific answers**.
- If you do not pass, you can **take the test again in future applications**.

More on percentiles is in [how scoring works](scoring.md#civil-service-judgement-test-percentiles).

## Practise with the official tools

GOV.UK links an unscored [CSJT practice test](https://eubusiness.panpowered.com/Civil_Service_Practice/CSJT_practice) and a [short video about the test](https://youtu.be/Vudzn3BqSXk). Take the practice test once to get used to the screens. Then use the [rating effectiveness items](sections/rating-effectiveness.md) and the [free situational judgement practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/sjt?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=situational-judgement-test) to build the habit.

## Do and don't

- **Do** take the test as early as you can, so there is time to sort out technical or access problems.
- **Do** find a quiet place, read all the instructions and answer every question.
- **Do** answer the self-assessment honestly.
- **Don't** ask anyone for input on your answers. GOV.UK says this directly.
- **Don't** close the browser unless you have to.
