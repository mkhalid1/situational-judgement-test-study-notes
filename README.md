# Situational Judgement Test Study Notes

A free, complete study guide for situational judgement tests (SJTs): how they work, one clear method that applies to every format, 28 original practice items with full explanations, how scoring really works, and study plans for a week or a weekend. Written for anyone facing an employer or graduate-scheme SJT, with a dedicated guide for the UK Civil Service Judgement Test and notes on the UCAT SJT for medical and dental applicants. Facts about named tests come only from GOV.UK and the UCAT, each one [cited](docs/sources.md).

Want to see where you stand first? Take the [free situational judgement practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/sjt?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=situational-judgement-test), or the shorter [15-minute diagnostic](docs/diagnostic.md) in this repo.

## Start here

1. **Find your weak formats.** Take the [15-minute diagnostic](docs/diagnostic.md). It has two items for each format and tells you where to start.
2. **Learn the five checks, then fix your weakest format.** Every guide uses the same frame, first set out in [most and least effective](docs/sections/most-and-least-effective.md#the-scoring-frame-used-in-these-notes). Then work through the guide for your weakest format.
3. **Follow a plan.** Use the [7-day plan](docs/study-plans.md#7-day-plan), or the [2-day plan](docs/study-plans.md#2-day-plan) if your test link has already arrived.

## Quick facts

SJTs are not one test. Every employer or admissions body uses its own. Here are two widely taken UK SJTs side by side:

| | Civil Service Judgement Test | UCAT Situational Judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Who takes it | Applicants for many UK Civil Service jobs | Applicants to UK medical and dental degrees |
| Length | Not timed. Most people take 2 to 4 minutes per scenario. Allow at least an hour | 69 questions in 26 minutes, plus 1 minute 30 seconds of instructions |
| Parts | Self-assessment (15% of the score), then scenarios with four actions each | Scenarios with up to six questions each |
| Answer scales | Counterproductive, Ineffective, Fairly Effective, Effective | Appropriateness and importance ratings (four points each), and most and least appropriate of three |
| Result | Percentile against a representative group of applicants | Band 1 (highest) to Band 4 |
| Partial credit | Not published | Yes: partial marks when a response is close to the correct answer |
| Pass mark | Set by the recruiter, and can be raised after the deadline | None published by the UCAT |
| Retakes | Allowed in future applications | Not covered in these notes |
| Official practice | Unscored practice test linked from GOV.UK | Question tutorials on the UCAT site |

Sources: [GOV.UK: Preparing for the Civil Service Judgement Test](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/preparing-for-the-new-civil-service-judgement-test) and [UCAT: Test format and scoring](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/about-ucat/test-format-and-scoring/), checked 24 November 2026. The UCAT says its tutorial information applies to the 2026 testing cycle and may change for 2027.

## The four formats at a glance

| Format | You decide | Answers | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Most and least effective | The best and the worst option | One "most" and one "least" from three to five options | [Most and least](docs/sections/most-and-least-effective.md) |
| Ranking responses | The order of four options | Ranks 1 to 4 | [Ranking](docs/sections/ranking-responses.md) |
| Rating effectiveness | How good each action is, on its own | Effective to Counterproductive, or Very appropriate to Very inappropriate | [Rating effectiveness](docs/sections/rating-effectiveness.md) |
| Rating importance | How much each factor should weigh in a decision | Very important to Not important at all | [Rating importance](docs/sections/rating-importance.md) |

Every format guide has the same parts: what it measures, how the questions look, the scoring frame, a step-by-step method, the traps that cost points, timing advice, 5 original practice items with explanations, and a score table.

## How to use this repo

- **Read on GitHub.** Every page has links to the previous page, the next page and back to these contents, so you can read it start to finish like a book.
- **Test yourself honestly.** Answers are hidden under "Answers and explanations". Commit to an answer before you open them.
- **Read every explanation,** including for items you got right. Most explanations say why the nearest wrong answer is wrong, which is where the learning is.
- **Keep a miss log.** For each miss, write which of the five checks you got wrong. The [study plans](docs/study-plans.md#how-to-review-a-missed-item) show how.
- **Practise timed.** These notes teach the method. A timed run, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/sjt?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=situational-judgement-test), teaches the pace.
- **Clone or fork it** if you want to annotate your own copy. The structure also works as a GitBook space: see [SUMMARY.md](SUMMARY.md).

## About the practice items

All 28 practice items (20 in the format guides and 8 in the diagnostic) are original, written for these notes. None are taken from the Civil Service Judgement Test, the UCAT, any employer's test or any prep site. They are set in offices, shops, hospitals, care homes, schools, public services and graduate schemes, and keyed against one stated frame, so that the reasoning you learn here transfers to the real test.

Real SJT keys come from panels of people in each role, so a real test may weigh a situation a little differently from these notes. Where that happens, follow the rules and role described in your own test's scenario.
